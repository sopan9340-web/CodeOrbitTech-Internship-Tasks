# Short Report: Phishing Email Identification

## Objective
To prepare a simple checklist for recognizing phishing emails and apply it to five fictional examples.

## Method
Each example was reviewed for sender/domain mismatch, urgency, suspicious links or attachments, unexpected prizes or payments, and requests for passwords or one-time passcodes.

## Findings
- Sample 1: Likely phishing — urgent account threat and password request.
- Sample 2: Likely phishing — unexpected prize and processing-fee request.
- Sample 3: High risk — unexpected executable-style attachment and threat.
- Sample 4: No obvious indicators in the text shown; independent sender verification is still sensible.
- Sample 5: Likely phishing — requests a password and one-time passcode under time pressure.

## Conclusion
Urgency, unknown or lookalike senders, unexpected attachments, and requests for confidential information are common warning signs. Users should avoid clicking suspicious links, verify requests through a separate trusted channel, and report suspected phishing messages.

## Limitation
These are fictional examples. Email text alone cannot conclusively establish whether a real email is genuine; sender and link details must be verified safely.
