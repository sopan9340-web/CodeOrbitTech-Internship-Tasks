# Five Fictional Sample Emails and Their Assessment

These examples are fictional and use reserved `.example` domains. Do not send real personal data to any address.

## Sample 1 — Suspicious account warning
**From:** `security-alert@paypaI.example`  
**Subject:** Your account will be locked in 10 minutes  
**Message:** “Confirm your password immediately using the link below or your account will be permanently closed.”

**Assessment:** Suspicious / likely phishing  
**Red flags:** Urgent threat, asks for a password, and the sender name contains a lookalike character.  
**Safe action:** Do not use the link. Open the official service directly and check account alerts.

## Sample 2 — Unexpected prize
**From:** `winner@free-prize.example`  
**Subject:** You won a new phone!  
**Message:** “You have been selected for a prize. Pay a small processing fee today to claim it.”

**Assessment:** Suspicious / likely phishing  
**Red flags:** Unexpected prize, payment request, urgency, and unknown sender.  
**Safe action:** Do not pay or share payment details.

## Sample 3 — Unexpected invoice attachment
**From:** `billing@unknown-vendor.example`  
**Subject:** Overdue invoice — open attachment  
**Message:** “Your payment is overdue. Open `invoice.scr` immediately to avoid legal action.”

**Assessment:** High risk  
**Red flags:** Unexpected attachment with an executable-style extension, pressure, and unfamiliar sender.  
**Safe action:** Do not open the attachment; verify with the vendor using known contact details.

## Sample 4 — Routine meeting notice
**From:** `colleague@company.example`  
**Subject:** Project meeting at 2 PM  
**Message:** “Hello, our project meeting is at 2 PM in the usual room. Please bring your notes.”

**Assessment:** No obvious phishing indicators in the text provided; sender still needs verification if unexpected.  
**Red flags:** None obvious from this sample alone.  
**Safe action:** Confirm the sender/domain if you were not expecting the message. Never rely on wording alone.

## Sample 5 — Payroll details request
**From:** `hr-payroll@company-payroll.example`  
**Subject:** Salary processing issue  
**Message:** “Reply with your password and one-time passcode so we can release your salary today.”

**Assessment:** Suspicious / likely phishing  
**Red flags:** Requests a password and one-time passcode, uses urgency, and uses a domain that may not be the employer's real domain.  
**Safe action:** Do not reply. Contact HR through a known internal channel.

## Summary
Samples 1, 2, 3, and 5 contain clear warning signs. Sample 4 has no obvious red flags in the supplied text, but should still be verified when unexpected. These assessments are educational and based only on the text shown.
