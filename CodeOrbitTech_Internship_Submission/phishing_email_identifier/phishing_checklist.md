# Phishing Email Identification Checklist

Use this checklist before clicking links, opening attachments, replying, or sharing information.

## Red flags
- **Unexpected sender:** the address is unfamiliar or uses a lookalike domain.
- **Urgent or threatening language:** claims your account will be closed immediately or demands instant action.
- **Suspicious links:** the visible text does not match the destination, or the domain is misspelled.
- **Unexpected attachments:** especially executable files or documents asking you to enable macros.
- **Requests for secrets:** passwords, one-time passcodes, payment details, or recovery codes.
- **Unusual payment requests:** gift cards, cryptocurrency, or changed bank details.
- **Generic greeting or odd writing:** can be a warning sign, though good grammar does not prove a message is genuine.
- **Too-good-to-be-true offers:** prizes, refunds, or rewards you did not expect.

## Safer response
1. Do not click links or open attachments.
2. Check the sender address carefully, not just the display name.
3. Visit the service using a known official app or address typed yourself.
4. Verify unexpected requests using a separate trusted channel.
5. Report the message to your email provider or IT/security team.
6. If you already entered a password, change it through the official service and enable multi-factor authentication.

**Important:** A single red flag is not always proof of phishing. Consider the whole message and verify independently.
